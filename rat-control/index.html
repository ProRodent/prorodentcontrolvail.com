Rats that live above the ceiling.

The rat most people picture lives at ground level, but the animal behind rooftop noise in warm climates is often a roof rat. It is a good climber that follows fences, block walls, wires and branches to reach an attic, and it prefers to nest high rather than low.

In Vail, plenty of lots border undeveloped ground. That does not mean your house has rats, and we do not assume it does. It does mean that the inspection looks at the lot edge and the roofline together, because animals can approach from either side.

Reading the evidence.

Rat droppings are noticeably larger than mouse droppings, and they often collect along the same path. Gnawing is bigger too, and a rat can open a hole in wood, vinyl or soft metal. Greasy rub marks along a beam or pipe show the route they run every night.

Tracing the route.

A rat job starts outside. The technician walks the lot, then the roof edge, then the attic, looking for the line that connects a food source to a way in. That line decides where traps go and which gaps get sealed first.

  • Look for gnawed edges around vents, soffits and roof returns.
  • Check where a wire or pipe runs from the roof or wall into the house.
  • Note any branch, cable or wall top that touches the roof.
  • Ask which part of the yard holds pet food, fallen fruit or feeders.

Removal and sealing.

Traps go where the droppings and rub marks point, and they are checked on a schedule. Rats are wary of new objects, so placement matters more than the number of traps. The trapping and removal page explains how that works in practice.

Sealing follows, and for rats it means serious materials. A gap of half an inch is enough, and a rat will chew through a soft patch, so we use metal and mesh instead of foam alone. The full list is on the exclusion page.

You do not need to know whether it is a mouse, a rat or a packrat. Say what you found and we will sort it out together.

Changes in the yard that help.

Outside, the goal is to take away food and cover. Pick up fallen fruit, store pet food and birdseed in sealed containers, and pull stacked material away from walls. Keep branches trimmed back from the roof so they cannot serve as a bridge.

Colossal Cave Mountain Park and the Rincon Mountains District of Saguaro National Park sit close to Vail, which is a reminder that wild habitat is near. If your lot backs onto open ground, tell us on the call, because the technician will check the boundary and any burrows before anything else.

If rats have already been in the attic, droppings and soiled insulation need safe removal. Our attic and crawl space cleanup page describes that step.

Safe handling checklist for droppings and nests

If you plan to clean before a visit, these CDC-based points are the safe way to do it. If you are unsure, wait and ask first.

  • Treat dry droppings as something to dampen first, not something to brush up.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Gloves on first, handwashing last. Do both every time.
  • Dampen droppings and urine stains with disinfectant or diluted bleach, leave it for about five minutes, then wipe with paper towels.
  • Put used towels and waste in a plastic bag, tie it closed and throw it away. Never touch a dead rodent without gloves.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Rat control questions

How do I tell a rat from a mouse?

A rat is much larger, leaves larger droppings and needs a bigger opening. Roof rats are agile climbers, so their activity often shows up in attics and along rooflines.

Do trees and fruit attract rats?

They can offer food and a climbing route. Fruit left on the ground and branches that touch a roof are the features that matter most.

Is it safe to clean up after rats myself?

Rodent waste can carry germs, so follow the CDC steps: air out the space, wear gloves and wet droppings before wiping. See a doctor if you feel unwell after exposure.

Will one visit end the problem?

Often the first visit removes animals and the sealing visit keeps new ones out. A return check confirms the traps are quiet and the seals are holding.

Can you work at night?

The line is answered around the clock, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows. Describe the activity and we plan from there.
