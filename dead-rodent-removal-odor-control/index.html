A smell with no obvious source.

A dead rodent in a wall, a ceiling or a low space produces a strong smell that can be hard to place. It often begins a few days after the animal dies and gets worse in warm weather. People describe it as sweet, sharp or like rotting food, and they often suspect plumbing first.

Southern Arizona summers speed all of this up. Heat makes odors stronger, and an attic or a wall void can trap them. If the smell is worse in the afternoon, that is a useful detail for the technician.

How the source is found.

The technician starts with your description and then follows the evidence. The strongest smell, flies near a vent, a stain spreading across a ceiling and warm spots in a wall all help narrow the search.

Why poison makes this worse.

Poison is a common reason for odor calls. An animal that eats it may leave the bait and die in a wall, a ceiling or a space you cannot reach. Federal rules limit consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations, and the EPA page linked below explains why.

If you have been using poison and now have a smell, tell the technician. It helps explain what is happening and where to look first.

Removal and treatment.

Once the source is found, the animal is removed and the area is cleaned and treated. If insulation, drywall or wood is soaked, it may be removed and replaced, because those materials hold odor. The technician shows you what was found.

  • Keep the area aired out while it clears.
  • Do not paint or seal over the spot until the source is gone.
  • Mention any stain that appeared on a wall or ceiling.
  • Ask whether insulation or drywall needs replacing.

After the source is out, the next steps are usually rodent exclusion to keep new animals from entering and attic and crawl space cleanup if soiled material remains.

Cleanup safety, drawn from CDC guidance

The CDC publishes cleanup steps for homes with rodent droppings or nests. This list restates the main points in plain words.

  • Treat dry droppings as something to dampen first, not something to brush up.
  • Let fresh air move through a stuffy garage or storage room for half an hour before you start.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Saturate the droppings and nearby surfaces with disinfectant. After about five minutes, take up the waste with paper towels.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • We cannot offer health advice. If symptoms appear after cleaning rodent waste, see a doctor and explain what you handled.

Odor questions

How long does the smell last?

It depends on the size of the animal, the temperature and how porous the material is. Removing the source ends the cause, and the smell usually fades over days to a couple of weeks.

Can I find it myself?

Sometimes, using your nose and a flashlight. If it is in a wall, ceiling or crawl space, a technician has the tools to reach it.

Will an air freshener work?

It masks the smell but does not remove the source. Removal is what actually solves the problem.

Why do flies show up?

Flies are drawn to decomposing material. A cluster near a vent, a wall or a ceiling stain is a clue to where the animal is.

Does poison cause this?

It can. A poisoned animal may die inside a wall, and that is one reason poison is a poor choice for indoor control.
