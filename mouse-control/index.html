How a mouse problem shows up in a Vail house.

Mice are the rodents most likely to live entirely inside a home. They nest in wall voids, behind appliances, in stored totes and in attic insulation, and they rarely travel far from food. A single pair can become a household problem faster than most people expect.

The signs are small. Droppings are about the size of a grain of rice, gnaw marks appear on packaging and soft plastic, and a mouse inside a wall tends to scratch for a few minutes and then go quiet. Because mice are mostly nocturnal, the first hint is usually a sound at night.

Vail sits at the edge of open desert, and many properties here are close to undeveloped ground. A dry setting does not stop mice, though. What a mouse needs is a gap, something to eat and a sheltered corner, and a house supplies all three in any climate.

Finding the route before a trap goes down.

A technician starts where the signs are strongest and works outward. Fresh droppings, rub marks and gnawing show the route, and the route shows the opening. Traps set without that information catch a few mice and miss the cause.

  • Check under the sinks, behind the stove and refrigerator, and around the water heater closet.
  • Look at the garage door corners, the weatherstripping and the line where the wall meets the slab.
  • Inspect the attic hatch, the edges of vents and any place wires or pipes pass through a wall.
  • Note stored cardboard, fabric and paper, which mice shred for bedding.

Trapping first, then sealing.

Snap traps and enclosed stations go along the routes the evidence shows, in spots that children and pets cannot reach. Mice are curious, so unbaited gaps along walls matter as much as the trap itself.

Sealing begins once the first traps show who is active. Steel wool alone is not enough, because mice pull it out. We use metal flashing, mesh, hardware cloth and sealant matched to each opening, and our exclusion service page lists the materials.

What to skip while you wait.

Do not sweep droppings dry, and do not scatter loose bait around the house. Dry cleanup lifts dust, and loose poison can end up in a pet or inside a wall where an animal dies. If you want to clean before the visit, follow the wet-cleanup steps in the CDC pages linked on this site.

Food storage helps a lot. Move dry goods and pet food into sealed containers, and lift bowls overnight. That does not solve the problem alone, but it removes the reward that keeps mice coming back.

After the traps come out.

A good result means no new droppings, no new noise and traps that stay empty. If an attic or wall void was heavily contaminated, attic and crawl space cleanup is the next step. If wiring or insulation was chewed, rodent damage repair scopes the fix separately.

If you are not sure whether you have mice or something larger, ask for an inspection first. Our inspection and estimates visit names the animal and ends with a plan.

Steps to take before touching droppings or nests

Rodent waste can carry germs, so how you clean matters. The points below summarize public CDC guidance.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests. That can put contaminated dust in the air you breathe.
  • Open doors or vents and let an enclosed space air out for 30 minutes or more before cleanup.
  • Put on gloves before touching anything contaminated, then wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.
  • Soak the droppings and the surface in disinfectant first. Wiping comes only after the soak time.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Symptoms such as fever, body aches or trouble breathing after contact with rodent waste call for medical advice. We cannot assess anyone's health.

Mouse control questions

How small a gap can a mouse squeeze through?

Roughly a quarter inch, about the width of a pencil. Pipe collars, door corners, dryer vents and the spot where a wire enters a wall all qualify.

Do mice live in a dry desert climate?

House mice live indoors wherever food and shelter exist, so a dry climate does not keep them out. Summer heat and monsoon rain can push them toward cooler, drier buildings.

Can I use store-bought poison in the meantime?

Consumer products are limited to ready-to-use bait stations, and poison can leave a mouse dying inside a wall. Ask the technician before you put anything down.

How long until the scratching stops?

It depends on how many mice are present and whether the entry points are sealed. The technician gives a realistic expectation after the inspection.

Do you take mouse calls at night?

Yes. The line is answered around the clock, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.
