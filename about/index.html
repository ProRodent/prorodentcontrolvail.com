How the work is organized.

Every job starts with an inspection. A technician looks at the attic, the garage, the yard and the roofline, names the animal and explains how it is getting in. You hear the plan and the price before work begins.

Trapping removes the animals that are inside. Sealing keeps the next ones out, and it is the part that lasts. Cleanup and repair come last, once nothing can get back in.

  • Inspection first, so treatment rests on what is actually there.
  • Traps and stations placed where children and pets cannot reach.
  • Entry points closed with materials rodents cannot chew through.
  • A follow-up check so the result is confirmed instead of assumed.

Rodenticide and safety.

Federal rules limit what consumers can buy for rodent control, and the EPA page linked below explains the restrictions. We prefer trapping and sealing, and we tell you plainly when a product is involved and where it sits.

Cleanup follows CDC guidance. Droppings and nests are wetted with disinfectant, not swept or vacuumed, and waste is bagged. We are not medical professionals and do not make health claims about anyone's exposure.

What to do before anyone arrives.

A few safe habits make the visit smoother and keep your household safer. They follow public health guidance, and they apply whether or not you call us.

  • Close the door on any room where you saw droppings or nests, and keep children and pets from wandering in.
  • Resist the urge to sweep or vacuum what you found, because dry cleanup puts dust in the air.
  • Hold off on loose bait or poison, since it complicates the inspection and can leave animals dying inside walls.
  • Jot down the hour and the room whenever you hear or see something, which helps trace the route.

Why we explain limits.

Some things cannot be promised. We cannot say how many animals are in a wall before looking, and we cannot promise that none will ever return. What we can do is find the openings, close them with durable materials and check that the work held.

We also say when something is not a rodent job. A bird in a vent, a squirrel in daylight or a noisy pipe each call for a different answer, and an honest answer saves you money.

  • We name the animal from evidence, not from a guess over the phone.
  • We separate trapping, sealing, cleanup and repair so you can see each cost.
  • We explain where traps and stations sit and why.
  • We leave you with notes on what was found and done.
