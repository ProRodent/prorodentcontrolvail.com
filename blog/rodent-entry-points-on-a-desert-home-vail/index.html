Entry Points on a Desert Home: Where Rodents Get In.

A mouse needs about a quarter inch and a rat about half an inch. This guide lists the places those gaps usually hide and what actually closes them.

A house that looks tight from the street can still have ten or twenty working entries. They are small, often hidden by trim or landscaping, and most people have never looked at them. A walk-around with a flashlight reveals more than you would expect.

The size rule.

A house mouse can pass through a gap of roughly a quarter inch, about the width of a pencil. A rat needs about half an inch. Those numbers are the starting point for any sealing plan, and they explain why pipe collars and worn door sweeps matter so much.

Where gaps usually are.

Location.What to look for.What closes it.
Roof edge and returns.Gaps under edge tiles, loose flashing.Metal flashing and stop strips.
Attic and wall vents.Torn or missing screens.Metal mesh or hardware cloth.
Pipe and wire entries.Rings of space around lines in a wall.Mesh packed in and sealed.
Garage door.Daylight under the bottom seal or at the corners.New seal and threshold repair.
Slab and foundation.Cracks, weep holes and utility entries.Mesh covers and sealant.
Doors and windows.Worn sweeps and gaps at the frame.New sweeps and weatherstripping.

A note on stucco, trim and desert landscaping.

If your home is stucco, gaps tend to form where the stucco meets trim, vents and pipes, so check those lines first. Palms, climbing vines and branches that touch the roof act as bridges for climbing animals. Trimming a few feet back is a cheap step that helps.

Vail is an unincorporated community in Pima County, and some communities have rules about exterior changes. If yours does, ask what is allowed before sealing anything that is visible. Tell the technician too, because it affects the materials.

How to do a safe walk-around.

  • Go in daylight with a flashlight and a notepad or phone.
  • Look up at the roofline, then along the walls, then down at the slab.
  • Photograph each gap with something for scale.
  • Do not poke into holes, because an animal may be inside.
  • Skip ladders on hot afternoons, and leave the roof for a professional.

Why materials matter.

Rodents chew. Foam, caulk and steel wool alone often fail, because teeth wear through them or animals pull them out. The reliable approach is a hard barrier, such as metal or mesh, with sealant to hold it in place.

That is why our exclusion and entry point sealing page lists metal flashing, hardware cloth and door sweeps rather than a single product. The right material depends on the gap.

Prioritizing what you find.

A walk-around may turn up a dozen gaps of different sizes. Not all of them are equally urgent, so it helps to sort them. Start with openings that show evidence of use, such as rub marks, droppings or gnawing, and work down to those that are merely open.

Priority.What it looks like.Why.
First.Rub marks, droppings or gnawing at the gap.Animals are already using it.
Second.A gap larger than half an inch with no evidence yet.A rat can pass through.
Third.A smaller gap with no evidence.A mouse could pass through, but nothing has yet.

A technician follows the same logic, and it is why an inspection can be faster than a long list of guesses. If you only have time for one step, make it the first row.

What to do with your findings.

If you find a gap, note its location and size and leave it open until any animals have been removed. A closed gap with an animal inside is a bigger problem than an open one. Call and describe what you found, and the technician decides the order.

A full inspection finds the openings that are in use, which may differ from the ones you spotted. After sealing, a return check confirms nothing was missed, and the steps in our trapping and removal page explain how animals inside are dealt with first.

Safe handling checklist for droppings and nests

Rodent waste can carry germs, so how you clean matters. The points below summarize public CDC guidance.

  • Resist the urge to sweep. Dry droppings crumble into dust that is easy to inhale.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Use disposable or washable rubber gloves, and wash both gloves and hands when you are done.
  • Dampen droppings and urine stains with disinfectant or diluted bleach, leave it for about five minutes, then wipe with paper towels.
  • Bag it, tie it, bin it. Bare hands stay away from dead rodents and anything they contaminated.
  • A fever or trouble breathing after rodent cleanup deserves a call to your doctor, with the exposure mentioned up front.

Things people ask next

Why do mice get in through such small gaps?

Their skulls and bodies compress. A gap about the width of a pencil is enough, and they use whiskers and touch to find it.

Is foam sealant enough?

Not on its own. Rodents chew through foam, so use metal, mesh or hardware cloth with sealant over it.

Are stucco homes more or less vulnerable?

Either way, gaps tend to form where stucco meets trim, vents and pipes. Check those edges on any home.

Should I seal gaps before or after trapping?

After the animals are out. Sealing first can trap animals inside the walls.

How often should I check?

Once each season is a good routine, plus after heavy storms. Keep photos so you can compare.
