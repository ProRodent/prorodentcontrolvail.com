Scratching in the Ceiling at Night: What to Do in Vail.

Noise overhead at night is how many rodent problems begin. Here is what the sounds usually mean, what to write down and what to leave alone.

It is 2 a.m., and something is moving above the bedroom ceiling. You hold your breath and it stops, then starts again. Almost everyone who hears this wants the same thing: to know what it is and whether it can wait until morning.

What the sounds usually mean.

Light, quick scratching often means mice, which are small and use wall voids and attic corners. Heavier thumping, running or dragging sounds suggest rats. A rolling or rattling sound may be an animal moving something around, and a chewing noise means exactly what it sounds like.

Sound.More likely cause.Note.
Light scratching that starts and stops.Mouse in a wall or attic.Often quiet for stretches.
Thumping or running overhead at dusk.Rat.Often follows the roofline or a beam.
Chewing or gnawing.Rodent working on wood or wire.Treat as a possible wiring concern.
Ticking and popping as temperatures change.Ducts or framing.Often follows the sun and cooling.

Other noises that mimic rodents.

Not every sound is a mouse or rat. Squirrels are active in daylight, birds can nest in vents and chirp, and pipes and ducts expand and contract. A technician will tell you honestly when the evidence points to something outside what we handle.

  • Daylight thumping suggests an animal that is active by day.
  • Chirping or fluttering near a vent suggests birds.
  • Ticking that follows temperature changes is usually the building.
  • Noises plus droppings, gnawing or rub marks point toward rodents.

What to write down tonight.

Details help a lot. Note the room, the time, the type of sound and how long it lasts. If you can, record a short audio clip on your phone. Do not go into the attic at night, because the light is poor and the heat may linger.

  • Which room the sound is above, and which direction it moves.
  • What time it started and how many nights it has happened.
  • Any droppings, smell or stains you have noticed.
  • Whether pets react to the noise.

What to leave alone.

Do not spread loose poison to quiet the noise. Poison can lead to an animal dying in a wall, and the smell can last weeks. Do not seal a hole you found without checking first, because closing a gap with an animal inside creates new problems.

If you want to deal with cleanup yourself later, follow the CDC steps in the safety box on this page: air out the space, wear gloves, wet droppings and wipe with paper towels.

A week-by-week view of what to do.

Noise rarely resolves itself, and acting early is easier than acting late. Here is a simple way to think about the first week after you notice something.

When.What to do.What to avoid.
Night one.Note the room, time and type of sound.Banging on the ceiling or going into the attic at night.
Days two and three.Check the house and garage for droppings, gnawing or smells.Sweeping anything dry or setting loose poison.
By day four.Call and describe what you have noted.Waiting for the noise to stop on its own.

A clear log of what you heard, when and where gives the technician a head start, and it often shortens the visit.

Safe handling checklist for droppings and nests

Nothing here is specific to our service. It is general cleanup safety, summarized from CDC guidance.

  • Do not use a vacuum on rodent waste, even a strong one. Wet it down first, as the steps below describe.
  • Before entering a closed space with signs of rodents, open it up and leave for about 30 minutes.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Wet droppings with a disinfectant or a bleach solution and let it soak for about five minutes before wiping with paper towels.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • We cannot offer health advice. If symptoms appear after cleaning rodent waste, see a doctor and explain what you handled.

More questions about this topic

What makes noise in a ceiling at night?

Mice, rats and sometimes other animals. Light scratching suggests mice, and heavier thumping suggests rats, though only an inspection can confirm.

Could it be something other than rodents?

Yes. Squirrels are active by day, birds can nest in vents, and ducts and pipes tick as the temperature changes.

Should I knock on the ceiling or poke around?

No. Disturbing an animal can send it deeper into the building. Note the sound and call.

Is it safe to sleep in that room?

It is usually a nuisance rather than an immediate danger, but you should avoid contact with droppings and nests. Call promptly if you notice a smell or stain.

What time of night is it usually active?

Mice and rats are mostly nocturnal, so many people hear them as they settle in for the night or before dawn.
