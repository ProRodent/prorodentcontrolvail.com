Why a business needs more than a quick fix.

A home has a few people and a few rooms. A business has customers, staff, deliveries, a stockroom and sometimes a kitchen. A rodent in any of those places can damage goods, upset customers and raise questions about hygiene.

That is why commercial work leans on records and routine. A written note after each visit shows what was found and what was done, and it is useful if a landlord, a manager or an inspector asks.

Plans, not just visits.

A single visit can clear a small problem. A site with food, frequent deliveries or a shared wall may need regular monitoring, so activity is caught early. The technician explains the options and you choose what suits your budget and risk.

  • Inspection of the whole building, inside and out.
  • Traps and monitoring stations placed along walls and entries.
  • Sealing of gaps found at doors, vents and utility lines.
  • A short note after each visit describing findings and actions.

What staff can do.

Staff are the first line of detection. A short checklist for opening and closing helps: look for droppings, gnawed packaging and new gaps, and report anything the same day.

Follow the CDC cleanup steps for droppings and do not sweep them dry. For the building itself, exclusion and entry point sealing closes the way in, and rodent trapping and removal deals with any animals found.

Cleanup safety, drawn from CDC guidance

Read this list before you touch anything that looks like droppings, urine stains or nesting material.

  • Do not use a vacuum on rodent waste, even a strong one. Wet it down first, as the steps below describe.
  • Let fresh air move through a stuffy garage or storage room for half an hour before you start.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Wet droppings with a disinfectant or a bleach solution and let it soak for about five minutes before wiping with paper towels.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • Tell a physician about any rodent exposure if flu-like symptoms or breathing problems follow. We are a pest service and cannot diagnose anything.
