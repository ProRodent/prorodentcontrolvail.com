Why repair is its own step.

Rodents gnaw to keep their teeth in check, and they gnaw whatever they find. In a house that can mean wiring, plastic pipe, ductwork, drywall and insulation. Repair is a different job from removal, and it is easier to plan once the animals are gone.

Doing repair first is a common mistake. If the openings are still open, new animals simply chew the fresh work. That is why the order is removal, sealing, cleanup and then repair.

Safety comes first with wiring.

Exposed or chewed wire is a safety concern. If you smell burning, see scorch marks or notice flickering lights, switch off the circuit if it is safe to do so and call an electrician. Do not touch the damage yourself.

  • Take photos before anything is moved or cleaned.
  • Do not run a vehicle or appliance with chewed wiring.
  • Note any warning lights, buzzing or burnt smell.
  • Keep a written list of what you saw and where.

Heat, monsoon and what they do to repairs.

Summer heat in southern Arizona is hard on materials and on the people working with them. The monsoon months from mid-June through September can bring heavy rain that finds any gap the animals left in a roof or wall. Mention any leak or stain when you call.

If a home is close to open ground, such as land near Colossal Cave Mountain Park or the Rincon Mountains District of Saguaro National Park, tell us. A repair is only worth doing once the way in is closed, and that is more likely to need attention for homes next to undeveloped land.

Steps to take before touching droppings or nests

This is general public-health guidance from the CDC, not a description of any one job. It applies whether you call us or anyone else.

  • Avoid any dry cleanup of droppings or nests, because disturbed waste turns into airborne dust.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Gloves are the minimum. Wash them while still on your hands, then wash your hands after taking them off.
  • Soak, wait about five minutes, then wipe. That order keeps dust down and gives the disinfectant time to work.
  • Seal everything you wipe up in a bag before it goes in the trash, and do not handle carcasses bare-handed.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Repair questions

What kinds of damage do rodents cause?

Chewed insulation, ducting, wiring, plastic pipe, screens and drywall are common. Droppings and urine also soil insulation and stored goods.

Is chewed wiring urgent?

It can be. Exposed wire may short and start a fire, so leave it alone, switch off the circuit if it is safe to do so and call a qualified electrician.

Do you repair wiring?

Wiring and electrical work should be handled by a qualified electrician. We identify and report the damage so you can hand it to the right trade.

Why separate repair from removal?

Removal and sealing come first so the damage is not repeated. Repair is then scoped on its own.

Will my insurance cover rodent damage?

Policies vary, and we cannot judge what yours covers. Photos and a written description of the damage help when you talk to your insurer.
